# Instagram approved caption system

This reference records the test package's copy direction after reviewing public captions from `homiestliving`, `norhor`, and `daaz_cn`. Refresh the sample review when stale. Learn from structural strengths without copying distinctive phrases, product names, claims, or brand voice.

## Target voice

Write concise editorial material stories that ordinary viewers can feel and designers or buyers can apply. Balance clear product naming and short rhythmic lines, recognizable home or body experience, and specific observations about material, light, proportion, texture, and application.

The result should sound like a knowledgeable person noticing a material, not a catalogue, generated mood text, or an academic design statement.

## Choose one mode per caption

### Product editorial

Use when the product name and visual identity are the strongest hook.

Typical movement: exact material or concept name → one short contrast or 2–3 tonal words → a lived-in moment → one visible material detail → one CTA.

### Human-use tension

Use when the image answers a recognizable problem or feeling.

Typical movement: concrete daily tension → body or room experience → how the material/application changes that experience → one CTA.

Never invent a customer, testimonial, performance result, or completed project.

### Material design

Use when texture, pattern, relief, sheen, proportion, or construction is the story.

Typical movement: one precise material observation → its effect on the furniture form or space → a plausible application direction → one B2B CTA.

Do not turn visible features into unsupported technical performance claims.

## Writing rules

- Default to natural US English unless the brief requests another language.
- Aim for roughly 220–450 characters; stay within the configured 600-character limit.
- Prefer short sentences and one longer explanatory sentence. Vary rhythm between posts.
- Name the material early when it helps recognition, but do not force the same opening every time.
- Describe only supplied facts and visible evidence such as hue, tonal variation, motif outline, pile direction, relief, seam, piping, drape, compression, light, and scale.
- Add interpretation, not a mechanical account of what appears in frame 1 and frame 2.
- Use exactly one CTA: fabric details, swatch/application discussion, or OEM/ODM conversation. A natural question is optional, not mandatory.
- Keep the disclosure compact: `Concept visualization using the supplied material reference.` or the approved brief wording.
- Use 3–5 specific hashtags. Prefer the material, application, and professional category over broad lifestyle tags.

## Language to avoid

Avoid repeated or unsupported use of `premium`, `luxury`, `timeless`, `elevate`, `best quality`, `perfect`, `revolutionary`, and `where X meets Y`. Also avoid:

- abstract adjective stacks without evidence;
- repeated `not just ... but ...` constructions;
- repeated em dashes and symmetrical AI-style contrasts;
- generic openings such as `A quiet corner, shaped by...`;
- slide narration such as `In the detail view...`;
- forced prompts such as `Would you explore this?` when a specific inquiry action is available;
- multiple CTAs in one caption;
- unrelated hashtag blocks.

## Claims boundary

Do not claim softness, comfort, durability, cleanability, water or stain resistance, sustainability, certification, fiber composition, MOQ, stock, capacity, price, lead time, or completed furniture/project status unless the source facts explicitly support it.

Do not copy another account's line, slogan, named product, metaphor, or distinctive paragraph structure. Borrow only high-level communication principles.

## Final review

Before approval, verify:

1. The opening is specific enough to remember.
2. At least one sentence refers to concrete visible evidence.
3. No sentence exists only to sound refined.
4. The caption does not narrate carousel mechanics.
5. There is one CTA, one disclosure, and no unsupported claim.
6. Hashtags are relevant and within the configured limit.
7. The caption is meaningfully different from recent project captions.

## Approved example

```text
CURLY FLEECE

Bold from across the room. Better up close.

The black-and-white loops give this rounded chair its character. Side light catches the curl, piping and seam—small details that change how the whole form reads.

Concept visualization using the supplied material reference.

For fabric details or OEM/ODM discussions, send us a DM.

#CurlyFleece #UpholsteryTextile #MaterialDesign
```

This example demonstrates the boundary; do not reuse its sentences for other materials.
